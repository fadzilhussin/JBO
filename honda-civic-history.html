The Honda Civic debuted in July 1972 as a compact, front-wheel-drive economy car — arriving right as the 1973 oil crisis made fuel efficiency a global priority. What began as a humble two-door fastback grew into one of the best-selling nameplates in automotive history, spanning eleven generations, multiple body styles, and technology that ranged from the emissions-beating CVCC engine to VTEC, hybrid powertrains, and the cult-favorite Type R.

Generation by Generation

Each redesign reflects the era it was born into — from the oil-crisis efficiency of the 1970s to today's hybrid and turbocharged lineup.

1st Generation

The Original Civic

1972 – 1979

Launched July 1972 as a two-door fastback sedan, soon joined by a three-door hatchback. Powered by a 1,169cc transverse engine with front-wheel drive. In December 1973, Honda introduced the CVCC engine, which met U.S. EPA emissions standards without a catalytic converter — a breakthrough during the fuel crisis.

2nd Generation

Growing Up

1979 – 1983

Released mid-1979 for the 1980 model year with a larger, more angular body. Engines grew to 1,335cc and an optional 1,488cc, both using refined CVCC "lean-burn" technology for better efficiency.

Larger bodyLean-burn CVCC
3rd Generation

The Wagon & the CRX

1983 – 1987

Introduced September 1983. This generation added the Civic Shuttle wagon and the lightweight CRX coupe. A new D-series 1.5L CVCC engine made 76 hp, alongside a high-performance 1.6L DOHC "ZC" engine rated at 130 hp.

4th Generation

VTEC Arrives

1987 – 1991

Released September 1987, larger and lower than before with fully independent rear suspension across the range. The sporty SiR trim introduced the B16A 1.6L DOHC VTEC engine — Honda's first mainstream use of variable valve timing.

B16A VTECSiR trimIndependent rear suspension
5th Generation

Efficiency Peak

1991 – 1995

Introduced September 1991 with more aerodynamic styling. The VX hatchback rated an astonishing 48/55 mpg (city/highway), Honda's most fuel-efficient Civic at the time. Offered both SOHC VTEC and VTEC-E variants.

VX hatchbackVTEC-EAero styling
6th Generation

Peak '90s Civic

1995 – 2000

Released September 1995. Added the natural-gas-powered Civic GX. Engine choices spanned SOHC D16Y7, SOHC VTEC D16Y8 (127 hp), and the DOHC VTEC B16A2 (160 hp) — a generation beloved by the tuner scene.

7th Generation

Hybrid & Type R Go Global

2000 – 2005

Released September 2000 with a flatter rear floor for more interior space, moving Civic into the "compact car" class. Suspension switched from double-wishbone to MacPherson struts. Debuted the Civic Hybrid (1.3L) and a redesigned Type R with i-VTEC.

8th Generation

Split Platforms

2005 – 2011

Launched September 2005 in North America with dramatically futuristic, angular styling. Sedan/coupe and hatchback versions split onto separate platforms, with the hatchback borrowing simplified rear suspension from the Honda Fit.

Futuristic dashDual platforms
9th Generation

Built Through a Crisis

2011 – 2015

Production began April 2011, developed in the shadow of the 2008 financial crisis with a sharp focus on affordability and fuel economy. A late-2012 refresh improved interior quality after early criticism. Never officially sold in Japan, aside from a limited 750-unit Type R run in 2015.

2012 mid-cycle refreshCost-focused
10th Generation

One World Civic

2015 – 2021

Unveiled September 2015 on an all-new global compact platform, unifying markets worldwide for the first time. Sleek fastback styling, a return of the three-door hatchback, and the first Type R officially sold in North America (imported from the UK).

11th Generation

Today's Civic

2021 – Present

Revealed as a prototype in November 2020, with the production version unveiled June 2021 and launched that fall. Built under Honda's "Man-Maximum, Machine-Minimum" philosophy: a longer wheelbase, rearward-shifted windshield pillars, and stiffer chassis. Offered as sedan and hatchback — the coupe was dropped due to falling demand. A refresh arrived for the 2025 model year (May 2024), and the range continues into the 2026 model year with sedan, hatchback, Si, hybrid, and Type R variants.

Signature Technology

A few innovations defined the Civic's engineering reputation across its five decades.

CVCC

Compound Vortex Controlled Combustion — introduced in 1973, it let the Civic meet strict U.S. emissions limits without a catalytic converter, years ahead of most competitors.

VTEC

Variable valve timing and lift, introduced on the 4th-generation SiR. VTEC became Honda's performance calling card through the '90s and 2000s tuner era.

Civic Hybrid

Launched on the 7th generation in 2001, it was one of the first mainstream hybrid sedans, later evolving into the current two-motor e:HEV system.

Type R

Honda's hardcore performance halo trim, first appearing on the Civic in 1997 (Japan). Today's 6th-gen Type R produces 330 PS from a 2.0L turbocharged K20C1.
